Before visualising or communicating data, analysts and data visualisation practitioners must understand the underlying structure, provenance, and reliability of the dataset. Because visual representations inherit all flaws, errors, and systematic biases present in raw measurements, critical evaluation of measurement error and sampling methodology is a prerequisite to effective visual communication.

We have already mentioned Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), a key task in the modern data visualisation pipeline. EDA is an iterative process in which we explore our data using analysis and visualisation to identify patterns, find anomalies, generate hypotheses and verify assumptions. EDA is a key step in data science workflows that allows us to determine *what* our data is telling us, before we move on to trying to communicate those findings to others. 

If we're going to carry out EDA, we need to understand our data, how it might be impacted by bias and error, and what methods and statistics we can use to explore it.
