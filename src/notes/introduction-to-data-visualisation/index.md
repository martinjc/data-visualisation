# {{ title }}

Data Visualisation is an increasingly important topic, and during this module we will combine consideration of the academic theory behind data visualisation with the practical skills of creating data visualisations. Over the course of the module we will consider what makes a 'good' data visualisation, and develop our own sense of aesthetic judgement and a critical eye for our own and other's work. This section serves to intoduce some of the high level ideas and topics that we will consider in more depth later on.

We'll start at the obvious beginning, by asking the question: **"What is Data Visualisation?"**

Jacques Bertin ([1967](https://search.worldcat.org/title/1102298170 "Semiology of graphics")) conceptualised the visual display of data as an artificial memory system that can support human perceptual capacities. He suggested that we could think of graphical encodings of data as an external storage mechanism to offset our own internal cognitive limitations. This suggests that data visualisation acts as a tool to support human cognition, providing us with the capability to observe patterns, trends, and relationships in data that might otherwise go unnoticed.

In modern computer science, Stuart Card, Jock Mackinlay, and Ben Shneiderman ([1999](https://archive.org/details/readingsininform0000card)) went further with their version of a formal definition, suggesting data visualisation is:

> _"The use of computer-supported, interactive, visual representations of data to amplify cognition."_

Again, here we see the idea that visualising data is not just about creating a visual representation of data, but about using that representation to support decision making and thought processes - it is not enough for our visualisation to simply represent the data in a visual fashion, it must also be effective at provoking thoughts and ideas.

Stephen Few ([2014](https://www.perceptualedge.com/)) further synthesises these perspectives, describing visualisation as the use of visual representations to explore, make sense of, and communicate data. It is exactly these tasks that we will focus on throughout this course
